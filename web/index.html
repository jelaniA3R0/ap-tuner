    Notch filter (motor noise)

    optional›

    Motors shake the gyro at their rotation frequency. A harmonic notch removes that noise so the rate loop can be tuned tighter. The app sets it up through the autopilot itself, because plain telemetry is too slow to carry the noise.

    In-flight FFT notch tune

    1. Enable the autopilot's FFT (one-time, needs a reboot while disarmed).
    2. Take off and hold a steady hover, sticks centred.
    3. Press Start measuring, wait 30 s, press Stop & read result. The autopilot writes the notch itself.

    How to use AP Tuner

    Flight tab: one guided flight (recommended)

    1. Connect and read. Press Start flight check. The app connects, reads the vehicle and runs the setup check. Take off and hover in Stabilize, AltHold or PosHold (QuadPlane: QStabilize / QHover / QLoiter; plane: FBWA).
    2. Check-up (30 s, nothing written). Tap the sticks. You get the issues per axis in plain words (sluggish, bouncy, oscillating, noisy D, motors at the limit…) and the exact parameter changes proposed, with the margins and the predicted response.
    3. Tune in flight. Press Apply in flight (or choose "Apply by itself" before starting). The gains go to the flying vehicle in steps of at most 1.5×, each watched 4 s for oscillation and reverted by itself if it trips. The app re-measures and repeats until the gains settle (at most 4 rounds per axis).
    4. Filters. The harmonic notch is set through the vehicle: ESC RPM tracking if the ESCs report RPM, otherwise the autopilot's own 30 s FFT measurement (FFT-capable boards with FFT_ENABLE = 1). If neither exists, you are told to set it from a log later. The gains of all chosen axes are then checked once more with the filter active.
    5. Overall check. 30 s of normal flying with the final gains: grade per axis, vibration, hover throttle, notch state, and whether the gains could still move.
    6. Land and save. Land and disarm. The summary compares the check-up with the final check, lists every write, and the parameter file is one click away.

    You can stop at any point; the Advanced tab keeps Undo per axis and Restore original gains.

    Telemetry + USB tab: for vehicles on a telemetry radio

    1. Connect Mission Planner over the radio as usual and turn on its mirror: Ctrl+F → Mavlink → TCP Host - 14550 → tick Write access → Connect. Press Start in AP Tuner.
    2. Take off and hover (QuadPlane: QStabilize / QHover / QLoiter). The 30 s capture starts by itself: short, sharp stick taps. Nothing is written in the air; the radio only marks the time.
    3. Land and disarm, then plug the USB cable into the autopilot (Mission Planner can stay on the radio). The app finds the port, downloads only the marked 30 s of the log at full rate and analyses it.
    4. Tick the changes and press Write to the autopilot. The old values go to an UNDO file first and every value is read back. Fly again and repeat until no change is proposed.

    No telemetry at all? Press No telemetry: go straight to USB after the flight: the longest hover in the log is used.

    Advanced tab: live tuning, step by step

    1. Connect. Press Connect; the app finds the vehicle by itself (Mission Planner mirror, simulator, USB or telemetry port). With Mission Planner open: Ctrl+F → Mavlink → TCP Host - 14550, tick Write access, Connect.
    2. Read the vehicle. Reads the tuning parameters and runs the setup check. It asks before setting the two housekeeping values it needs (PID telemetry on, slew limit non-zero).
    3. Tune. Choose the feel and axes, press Start, fly in Stabilize / AltHold (planes: FBWA) with short sharp stick taps. After each 30 s window you get a proposal with the predicted and measured response. Press Apply, or switch on Auto-apply.
    4. Notch filter. Check what is possible: ESC RPM tracking (best, if your ESCs report RPM), the autopilot's in-flight FFT (Cube Orange class boards), or a log with raw gyro data.
    5. Save. Download the parameter file for your records, or load it on another aircraft of the same build.

    How do I know it will not apply wrong gains?

    • Nothing is written unless you press Apply (or switch Auto-apply on). The only exceptions are the two housekeeping values in step 2, and it asks first.
    • Every proposal is derived from what the vehicle actually did: it fits a physical model to the measured response and refuses to propose when the measurement is weak (and tells you why).
    • Before writing, you see the exact parameters, the margins (gain margin, phase margin, sensitivity peak) and the predicted step response next to the measured one.
    • Changes are capped at 2× per round, written in steps of at most 1.5× and watched 4 s each by an oscillation detector (the same slew-rate signal ArduPilot's QuikTune uses). Any sign of oscillation reverts to the last good gains and pauses.
    • Undo per axis and Restore original gains are always available, and every write is journaled in the sessions folder.

    What the maths does

    From demanded rate, actual rate and the PID output it measures the bare airframe response (actuator → gyro) with a feedback-safe spectral estimator and fits G(s) = K·e−s·Td / ((s + a)(τs + 1)): K is how hard the motors turn the aircraft, a the aerodynamic damping, τ the motor lag, Td the delay. It then searches P and D against ArduPilot's exact AC_PID structure (target/error/D filters, the gyro low-pass, notches, loop rate) for the best disturbance rejection within the chosen margins, and sets I, FF, filters and the angle gain with ArduPilot's own rules (I = P on roll/pitch, I = 0.1 P on yaw, plane I = FF, FLTT = FLTD = INS_GYRO_FILTER/2, plane FF from the AUTOTUNE event method). Copter gains are computed per rad/s, so the numbers are directly ArduPilot's.

    Telemetry speed

    A copter's rate loop needs about 50 Hz of PID data for a trustworthy model: USB, Wi-Fi/Ethernet links and fast radios give that. A 57600 baud radio gives about 15 Hz, enough for diagnostics and cautious proposals; use the Log file tab for the precise work. Planes are slower, so 25 Hz is fine for feed-forward and the time constant.

    Notch filter from a log

    Set INS_RAW_LOG_OPT = 9 (Cube Orange / H7 boards) or INS_LOG_BAT_MASK = 1, INS_LOG_BAT_OPT = 4, fly 1-2 minutes including a steady hover, then analyse the log. You see the noise spectrum, the recommended INS_HNTCH_* values, the predicted result and the phase lag. Clear the logging option afterwards.

    Testing without a vehicle

    In the sitl folder run run-sitl.cmd, then fly test quad.cmd; press Connect here. The simulated quad takes off and taps the sticks for you.

    Safety

    Tune in calm air at a safe height, one axis at a time, with a vehicle that already hovers. Keep a hand on the mode switch. The app changes only the rate PID / filter / angle-gain parameters of the chosen axes, the notch parameters in step 4, GCS_PID_MASK and *_SMAX.
